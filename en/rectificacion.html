What is spinal flattening?

Spinal flattening is the reduction or loss of the physiological curve (lordosis) in the neck or low back. Imaging may show a “straighter” spine; clinically it means stiffness and fatigue.

What symptoms are linked to spinal flattening?

It often relates to sustained postures, stress, limited thoracic mobility, protective patterns after pain or training habits. It does not always hurt, but it can predispose to overload.

If it persists, neck or shoulder tension, standing low-back discomfort and thoracic or pelvic compensations can increase. Tolerance to sedentary work may also drop.

How is spinal flattening treated at Kinésica?

At Kinésica we prioritize RPG and muscle-chain work to restore postural organization and mobility. We integrate osteopathy and manipulations when restrictions block a functional curve.

How is spinal flattening assessed?

Assessment covers posture, segmental mobility and control. Sessions work on breathing, dosed extension and load strategies so your spine tolerates daily life better.

Related complications

  • Neck or low-back stiffness
  • End-of-day postural fatigue
  • Pain with screens or standing
  • Thoracic or pelvic compensations
  • Recurrent muscle spasm

When should you seek care for spinal flattening?

This information is educational and does not replace medical assessment.

  • Severe pain that does not improve with relative rest or worsens quickly.
  • Progressive weakness, sensory changes, or difficulty walking.
  • Recent trauma, fever, general malaise, or new unusual symptoms.

Frequently asked questions

What does cervical spine rectification (loss of lordosis) mean?

It means the natural C-shaped forward curve (lordosis) of the neck has flattened into an abnormally straight, rigid vertical alignment.

What symptoms commonly accompany cervical rectification in daily life?

A persistent sensation of heavy stiffness across the shoulders and neck, restricted rotation, tension headaches by day's end, and visual fatigue.
