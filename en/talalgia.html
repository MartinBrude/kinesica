What is heel pain?

Talalgia is pain around the heel, often felt with weight-bearing, walking or first steps in the morning. It may affect one side or both.

What symptoms are linked to heel pain?

Typical contributors include load increase (more walking or running), ankle stiffness, posterior-chain tension and altered plantar support. Footwear and surfaces can also influence symptoms.

If it continues, it can change your gait, create knee or hip discomfort through compensation and reduce physical activity. Morning pain and tissue irritability often increase.

How is heel pain treated at Kinésica?

At Kinésica we use manual therapy for the foot and ankle, osteopathy to improve mobility and load distribution, and targeted manual manipulations when indicated. We integrate muscle-chain work and RPG to address overall gait mechanics.

How is heel pain assessed?

Assessment includes plantar support, ankle mobility and load tolerance. Sessions progress with foot and calf strengthening, activity dosing and a plan to return to walking or running.

Related complications

  • Altered gait pattern
  • Compensatory knee pain
  • Persistent calf tightness
  • Reduced training tolerance
  • Increased morning pain

When should you seek care for heel pain?

This information is educational and does not replace medical assessment.

  • Severe pain that does not improve with relative rest or worsens quickly.
  • Progressive weakness, sensory changes, or difficulty walking.
  • Recent trauma, fever, general malaise, or new unusual symptoms.

Frequently asked questions

What causes sharp heel pain when taking the first steps out of bed?

It is typically triggered by microtrauma and stiffness in the plantar fascia and Achilles insertion at the calcaneus, which suddenly stretch after being contracted overnight.

Is a calcaneal spur visible on an X-ray always the root cause of heel pain?

No. Heel spurs are frequently asymptomatic incidental findings. The real pain source is almost always inflammation or strain within the plantar fascia and surrounding soft tissues.
