What is dental occlusion?

Dental occlusion describes how teeth meet on closing. When contact is uneven, incomplete or forced, the TMJ and masticatory muscles can overload.

What symptoms are linked to dental occlusion?

It may link to bruxism, tooth wear, recent dental work, jaw tension or cervical compensations. Pain can be felt in the jaw, temple, ear or neck.

Without care, clicking, tension headaches, limited opening and chewing fatigue can increase. Head and shoulder posture often feed the tension cycle.

How is dental occlusion treated at Kinésica?

At Kinésica we treat the TMJ with specific techniques, osteopathy and gentle manipulations, plus RPG when posture perpetuates overload. We coordinate with your dentist or orthodontist when needed.

How is dental occlusion assessed?

Assessment covers opening, mandibular movement quality, muscle tension and links to neck and posture. We set goals for relief, mobility and habits (clenching, sleep, screens).

When should you seek care for dental occlusion?

This information is educational and does not replace medical assessment.

  • Severe pain that does not improve with relative rest or worsens quickly.
  • Progressive weakness, sensory changes, or difficulty walking.
  • Recent trauma, fever, general malaise, or new unusual symptoms.

Frequently asked questions

What types of malocclusion exist and how do they connect to posture?

Crossbites, open bites, and deep overbites alter jaw positioning; through fascial connections and myofascial chains, these bite imbalances affect cervical alignment.

Why does dental malocclusion frequently provoke recurrent neck pain or headaches?

Uneven tooth contact forces masticatory and suboccipital muscles into continuous compensatory contractions to swallow comfortably and balance head posture.
