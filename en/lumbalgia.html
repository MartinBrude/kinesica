What is low back pain?

Low back pain is discomfort in the lower spine area. It may start after an effort, from sustained posture, or without a clear trigger, and it often fluctuates depending on activity.

What symptoms are linked to low back pain?

It is commonly linked to muscle overload, hip or spinal stiffness, low tolerance to certain positions, and reduced trunk coordination. Stress and sleep also influence how pain is experienced.

If not addressed, it can lead to movement avoidance, deconditioning and repeated episodes. In some cases it is associated with discomfort into the buttock region or increased sensitivity around the lumbar spine.

How is low back pain treated at Kinésica?

At Kinésica we combine manual therapy and integrated postural work: osteopathy and manual manipulations when indicated, plus RPG and muscle-chain work to improve loading patterns and mobility. The aim is better movement, not only temporary relief.

How is low back pain assessed?

Assessment looks at what aggravates and eases symptoms, hip mobility, trunk control and daily habits. Sessions include progressive exercise, education to manage flare-ups and a clear return-to-activity plan.

Related complications

  • Recurring flare-ups
  • Stiffness and functional limitation
  • Referred buttock pain
  • Fear of movement
  • Low tolerance to sitting or standing

When should you seek care for low back pain?

This information is educational and does not replace medical assessment.

  • Severe pain that does not improve with relative rest or worsens quickly.
  • Progressive weakness, sensory changes, or difficulty walking.
  • Recent trauma, fever, general malaise, or new unusual symptoms.

Frequently asked questions

What are the most frequent causes of lower back pain?

Lower back pain is commonly linked to facet joint stress, lumbar muscle guarding, hip stiffness (tight psoas and hamstrings), and poor endurance in deep core stabilizing muscles.

Why does low back pain worsen when standing up after prolonged sitting?

Prolonged sitting elevates intradiscal pressure and shortens hip flexors. Upon standing, the lumbar spine experiences sudden compressive tension that triggers reactive muscle spasms.

How is low back pain assessed at Kinésica?

Assessment looks at movements that aggravate or ease symptoms, hip and spine mobility, trunk control, and daily habits to build a clear progressive care plan.

Can you return to activity and exercise with low back pain?

Yes. Care focuses on restoring movement and strength with progressive exercise, load adjustment, and practical education to regain confidence in daily activity.
