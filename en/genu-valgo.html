What is knee valgus?

Knee valgus describes an alignment where the knees tend to drift inward, especially during squats, landing and stairs. It may or may not be painful.

What symptoms are linked to knee valgus?

It is often linked to hip control (glutes), ankle mobility, plantar support and learned movement patterns. Fatigue and higher loads can make it more noticeable.

When painful, it can increase stress on the knee, contribute to kneecap or inner-knee symptoms and create compensations at the hip or foot. It may also affect sport performance.

How is knee valgus treated at Kinésica?

At Kinésica we integrate muscle-chain work and RPG to improve postural organization, alongside hands-on care. We use osteopathy and manual manipulations when indicated to optimize hip and ankle mobility and support a better loading pattern.

How is knee valgus assessed?

Assessment includes squat, landing and gait analysis. Sessions progress with glute strengthening, neuromuscular control and sport- or activity-specific drills.

Related complications

  • Patellofemoral pain
  • Inner knee overload
  • Higher injury risk from poor control
  • Foot and ankle compensation
  • Fatigue with running or jumping

When should you seek care for knee valgus?

This information is educational and does not replace medical assessment.

  • Severe pain that does not improve with relative rest or worsens quickly.
  • Progressive weakness, sensory changes, or difficulty walking.
  • Recent trauma, fever, general malaise, or new unusual symptoms.

Frequently asked questions

What are the biomechanical causes of knock-knees (genu valgum)?

It can be developmental or arise from weakness in the hip abductors and external rotators, compounded by excessive inward foot pronation.

Which neighboring joints suffer most from knee valgus alignment?

The feet (forced into pronation), the patellofemoral joint (due to lateral patellar tracking), and the lateral meniscus under elevated compressive loads.
