Ciatalgia

Ciatalgia often feels like pain, burning or tingling down the leg. It doesn’t always mean a serious injury, but it does need a clear assessment.

  • What it is. Ciatalgia refers to symptoms that follow the sciatic nerve pathway: pain, electric sensations, tingling or numbness in the buttock, thigh or leg.
  • Symptoms. It may come from irritation near the lumbar spine, tension in pelvic tissues, or a mix of stiffness, load and sensitivity.

What is ciatalgia?

Ciatalgia refers to symptoms that follow the sciatic nerve pathway: pain, electric sensations, tingling or numbness in the buttock, thigh or leg.

What symptoms are linked to ciatalgia?

It may come from irritation near the lumbar spine, tension in pelvic tissues, or a mix of stiffness, load and sensitivity. Sustained postures and certain efforts commonly trigger it.

If it progresses, it can limit walking, sleep or sitting, and lead to compensations that overload the low back. Nerve sensitivity may increase and prolong episodes.

How is ciatalgia treated at Kinésica?

At Kinésica we treat both the cause and the nerve’s environment: neurodynamics to improve mobility and tolerance, osteopathy and manual manipulations when indicated, and RPG to reorganize posture and unload the lumbar region.

How is ciatalgia assessed?

Assessment includes neurological and movement tests to choose the right stimulus without pushing through. Sessions progress with exercise, flare-up education and a safe return-to-activity strategy.

Related complications

  • Persistent sitting pain
  • Reduced walking tolerance
  • Recurrent tingling
  • Lumbar and pelvic compensations
  • Strength loss from avoidance

When should you seek care for ciatalgia?

This information is educational and does not replace medical assessment.

  • Severe pain that does not improve with relative rest or worsens quickly.
  • Progressive weakness, sensory changes, or difficulty walking.
  • Recent trauma, fever, general malaise, or new unusual symptoms.

Frequently asked questions

Why does sciatic nerve pain radiate down the back of the thigh to the leg and foot?

The sciatic nerve is the body's longest nerve, formed by lumbar and sacral nerve roots. Mechanical compression or chemical irritation anywhere along its path refers pain down its entire anatomical pathway.

What is the difference between true disc sciatica and piriformis syndrome?

Disc-related sciatica involves nerve root impingement at the lumbar spine; piriformis syndrome is an entrapment of the nerve lower down in the buttock caused by a tight piriformis muscle.

Does sciatica always mean a serious injury?

Not always. Sciatic pain can be linked to mechanical irritation and needs a clinical assessment to identify the source and guide treatment decisions.

What does a sciatic pain assessment include at Kinésica?

We assess lumbar spine and hip function, neural mobility, and symptom triggers to combine manual therapy, neurodynamics, and progressive exercise.
